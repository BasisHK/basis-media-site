02 · Podcast

後生仔講嘢 / Youngblood

A bilingual founder show built from zero — production, clips, and the social engine that carried it into the Top 20 HK Business charts.

Podcast

The brief

Two young Hong Kong founders wanted a podcast. What they actually had was a Google Doc of guest names and a strong opinion that every HK business show sounded like a bank advert. They were right. The category was full of people in blazers saying nothing carefully.

The brief: build a show that sounds like how founders actually talk in this city — switching between Cantonese and English mid-sentence, swearing about landlords, arguing about rent per square foot. And build it as a system, not a hobby, so it survives episode six.

What we made

Full end-to-end production: format design, studio setup in Kwun Tong, two-camera recording, edit, mix, artwork, and the release calendar. We locked a 38-minute runtime and a cold open that drops you into the sharpest 20 seconds of the conversation before any intro music plays.

The growth engine mattered more than the episode. Every recording produced nine vertical clips, three quote cards and one long-form YouTube cut. Bilingual subtitles on everything — Cantonese audio, English burn-in and vice versa, because the HK audience is genuinely split and most shows pick one and lose half. AI-assisted transcription and clip-hunting meant we could turn a Tuesday record into a full week of assets by Wednesday night.

The result

The show hit the Top 20 HK Business chart by episode 11 and settled into 60k monthly listens across audio and video. Clips outperformed the episodes as a discovery channel by roughly 6:1 — the pattern we now build into every podcast we take on.

More usefully for the founders: three of their guests became clients of their day business. A podcast is a business development tool wearing a microphone.
