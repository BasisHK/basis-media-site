01 · Short-form video

Kowloon Noodle Co.

Twelve videos. One shoot day. A Sham Shui Po noodle shop that now has a queue down Fuk Wing Street every Saturday.

Short-form video

The brief

Kowloon Noodle Co. had been pulling noodles in the same Sham Shui Po shopfront for thirty-one years. The regulars knew. Nobody under 30 did. Weekends were the problem — the lunch trade held, but Saturday and Sunday, the tables sat empty while queues formed outside newer, louder places two streets away.

The brief we wrote after two afternoons in the shop was one line: make the internet see what the regulars already know. No rebrand. No new menu. Don't touch the recipe. Just point a camera at the thing that's already good.

What we made

A 12-video vertical sprint, all shot in a single day, all cut hook-first. The star wasn't the food — it was 老闆 Wong's hands. Forty seconds of dough being slammed, folded, pulled into strands thinner than the steam behind them. We shot it at 120fps, cut it to 9 seconds, and let the sound do the work: no music, just the slap of dough on stainless steel.

Around that hero we built the supporting cast — the 4am prep run, the regular who's ordered the same bowl since 1998, the sceptical nephew tasting the broth. AI storyboarding gave us twenty hook variants before lunch; we killed nineteen. Every asset shipped vertical-first with burned-in bilingual captions, because half the audience watches with the sound off on the MTR.

The result

The dough video crossed a million views in nine days without a dollar of paid spend. Total sprint reach hit 2.4M. But the number that mattered was on the floor: weekend covers up 180%, sustained across the following quarter, with a wait list that now starts at 11:40am.

Kowloon Noodle Co. hired a second front-of-house staffer. That's the metric we care about.
