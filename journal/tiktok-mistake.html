Why your restaurant's TikTok should look like a mistake

The glossy food film your cousin's agency shot is invisible. Hand-held, badly lit, one honest sentence from the boss — that's the clip that ends up in a group chat at 11pm.

4 min readBasis Media HK

We sat through a tasting menu last month that cost more than our camera kit. The food was precise. The room was quiet in the way expensive rooms are quiet. Then the owner opened TikTok to show us “the content” — a 45-second reel with a drone shot of Central, a slow pour of soy, soft jazz, and a caption that could have belonged to any of twelve other restaurants on the same street.

It had 800 views. Most of them from staff.

Meanwhile a dai pai dong in Sham Shui Po had posted a vertical clip of the boss yelling at a wok, slightly overexposed, Cantonese only, ending on a plate that looked like it might burn your fingers. 180,000 views. Comments from people who’d never been to Kowloon asking for the address.

Polish is a filter people have already learned to skip. Imperfection still feels like a person was in the room.

What “mistake” actually means

We’re not arguing for bad craft. We’re arguing against the kind of finish that signals “ad” before the first cut lands. In Hong Kong short-form, the clip that travels is the one that feels like it was filmed by someone who eats there — not commissioned by someone who markets there.

That usually means:

  • Hand-held, or a phone on a sticky pad near the pass
  • Available light, even if it’s a bit yellow
  • One honest line from whoever owns the place
  • A ending that is a plate, a receipt, or a queue — not a logo sting

The “mistake” is the texture. A mic bump. A laugh that wasn’t scripted. A subtitle that corrects itself mid-sentence because the boss changed his mind. Audiences clock that as real faster than they clock your colour grade.

Field note

If your agency still leads with a hero film and “social cutdowns,” ask them what the first three seconds look like with the sound off on a packed MTR. If the answer is a logo, rewrite the brief.

Why glossy dies in the feed

Feeds are full of competent beauty. Competent beauty is background radiation. The algorithm doesn’t hate it — people do, quietly, by swiping. A restaurant’s advantage isn’t production value. It’s proximity: steam, noise, a person who knows the dish by touch.

When we shoot F&B now, we treat the first nine seconds like a tip from a friend. Not a brand film. If it couldn’t survive being AirDropped into a WhatsApp group, it isn’t done.

A small test you can run this week

Film the same dish two ways. Once with the “proper” setup. Once with the phone, the owner, and one instruction: say what you’d tell a regular who asked what to order. Post both. Don’t boost either. Read the comments, not the vanity metrics.

Almost every time, the second one gets saved. Saved is the quiet signal that someone plans to come.

That’s the whole thesis. Look a little wrong on purpose — so you sound like you belong on the street you’re trying to fill.
